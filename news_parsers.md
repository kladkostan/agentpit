# Best News APIs for a Low-Volume, Recency-Focused Keyword Bot (2026)

## TL;DR
- **For your exact profile (150–300 keyword requests/day, a few every ~15 min, real-time-ish, free, open API), the single best default is the Currents News API** — 1,000 requests/day free, real keyword + Boolean search, real-time multi-source coverage, instant key with no credit card, and no documented per-second hard cap that would IP-block you. Pair it with **The Guardian Open Platform** (the most lenient and predictable rate limits of any option: 12 req/sec, 5,000/day) as a rock-solid secondary, and use **GDELT DOC 2.0** (free, keyless, unlimited but burst-sensitive) only as a throttled breadth/recency supplement.
- **Skip these "free" options for this use case:** NewsAPI.org (100/day, 24-hour delay, development-only — three disqualifiers), GNews free (100/day < your 150–300/day need, 1 req/sec, non-commercial), mediastack free (100 requests/**month**, HTTP-only, 30-min delay), Bing News Search (retired August 11, 2025), Brave Search API (free tier eliminated February 2026), and Reddit (now requires pre-approval for all access).
- **Recommended fallback chain:** Currents → Guardian → NewsData.io → GDELT (throttled) → niche supplements (Hacker News Algolia for tech, Spaceflight News API for space). This degrades gracefully when any single source is rate-limited or down.

## Key Findings
- **Your volume is small enough that daily caps barely matter, but per-second/burst limits and recency are decisive.** At 150–300 requests/day spread across 15-minute cycles you average well under 0.01 req/sec, so any API with a ≥500/day quota and a soft (or absent) per-second limit is comfortable.
- **GDELT's reputation for IP-blocking is real but is a burst phenomenon.** The GDELT Project blog states verbatim that "Our APIs are rate limited to protect the underlying ElasticSearch clusters, given the enormous volume of requests we receive during peak events," and it returns HTTP 429s; per GitHub issue #22 of the `alex9smith/gdelt-doc-api` client, "the API now needs a user agent before returning any data." At your spacing, with one request at a time and backoff, it is usable — but its unpredictability makes it a poor *sole* primary.
- **The most lenient, predictable, truly-soft rate limit belongs to The Guardian** (12 calls/sec, 5,000/day, free developer key), but it is a single publisher.
- **The best real-time multi-source free aggregator is Currents** (1,000/day, real-time, Boolean search). **NewsData.io** is the best free aggregator that explicitly permits commercial use (200 credits/day ≈ 2,000 articles, but a 12-hour delay on free).
- **Several "free" APIs are disqualified by hard limits**: see the Caveats/Skip list.

## Details

### The strong picks
**Currents API (currentsapi.services).** The closest match to all your "strongly preferred" criteria simultaneously. Per the official Currents site, "Pricing starts at $0 for the free tier (1,000 daily requests)… No credit card required to sign up," and it covers "120,000+ sources across 70+ countries and 20+ languages" with "99.9% uptime." The key is issued instantly, and the search endpoint supports both a simple `keywords` parameter and a full Boolean `query` parameter (AND/OR/NOT, quotes, parentheses). It is real-time with explicit categories for politics (`politics_government`), finance/business (`economy_business_finance`), sports (`sport`), and tech (`science_technology`). Crucially for your pain point #1, **no per-second or per-minute hard cap is documented** — only the daily quota — so there is no published burst-block behavior like GDELT's. The honest caveats, verified from its Terms of Service (last updated December 27, 2025): the free tier returns "partial access to news articles" (title/description/metadata, not full body text — full text is a paid feature), the ToS restricts creating permanent databases, reselling, or redistributing the article data without written permission, and an attribution ("Powered by Currents News API") is required unless waived. For a bot that *reads* news to inform trading decisions (rather than republishing the articles), this is workable, but confirm there is no hidden burst cap after you sign up.

**The Guardian Open Platform.** If your pain point #1 (no aggressive rate limiting) is paramount, this is the gold standard. The limits are confirmed directly by the Guardian Open Platform team on the official `guardian-api-talk` forum: "usage is restricted to 5000 calls per day and a maximum of 12 per second," and the team notes "we have recently revised our rate limits… the response headers will include your total rate limits (per day and per minute) as well as your remaining limits" — i.e., a genuinely soft, transparent limit. It is real-time, returns the **full article body text** (rare among free APIs), and covers politics, finance, sports, and tech well. The two limits are that it is a *single publisher* (so it can't corroborate across outlets or cover, e.g., niche US sports the way an aggregator can) and the ToS asks that you not cache Open Platform content for more than 24 hours. The key arrives by email instantly.

**GDELT DOC 2.0.** Free, keyless, effectively unlimited daily volume, with the best keyword/Boolean search and 15-minute freshness of anything here. The catch is exactly what you flagged: the GDELT Project blog confirms "Our APIs are rate limited to protect the underlying ElasticSearch clusters," it returns 429 "rate limit" responses and can throttle/block bursty IPs, and (per GitHub issue #22 of the `alex9smith/gdelt-doc-api` client) it now requires a browser-like `User-Agent` header — requests without one are rejected. At your volume — a few spaced requests every 15 minutes, one at a time, with exponential backoff on 429 — it works, but its unpredictability under load makes it a supplement rather than a sole primary. For high volume GDELT itself recommends the downloadable Web NGrams 3.0 dataset instead of the API.

**NewsData.io.** The best free aggregator that *explicitly allows commercial use*. Per NewsData.io's official blog, free users get "200 [credits]… 10 articles per credit… 2000 articles per day," with a rate limit of "30 credits per 15 minutes" — which lines up neatly with your 15-minute schedule — and the docs state "The Free News API can also be used for commercial purposes." It supports keyword search with language/country/category filters. The downsides on free: a **12-hour delay** on data (a real handicap for fast-moving prediction markets, though fine for "last few days") and a 100-character query limit.

### The verticals
- **Finance/business:** **Marketaux** (100/day free) resolves articles to stock tickers and adds sentiment — ideal for the finance vertical specifically, though too finance-narrow and too low-cap for general use.
- **Tech:** **Hacker News Algolia API** is keyless, free, has no practical rate limit for spaced use, and supports `search_by_date` for chronological recency — an excellent free tech supplement.
- **Space (if a market topic):** **Spaceflight News API** is keyless, free, open, commercial-friendly, with a `search` parameter and `ordering` for recency.

### Status verifications you asked for
- **Bing News Search / Bing Search APIs: retired August 11, 2025.** Microsoft's official Learn lifecycle announcement states verbatim: "Bing Search APIs will be retired on August 11, 2025. Any existing instances of Bing Search APIs will be decommissioned completely, and the product will no longer be available to be used or new customer signup." Endpoints now return 410/401. The only Microsoft path is "Grounding with Bing Search" inside Azure AI Agents — an agent-only, paid, more expensive tool, not a drop-in REST news API. **Do not build on Bing.**
- **NewsAPI.org free tier confirmed from the live pricing page:** Developer plan = **100 requests/day, articles delayed 24 hours, search up to 1 month old, CORS for localhost only, "development and testing… only, cannot be used in a staging or production environment (including internally)."** Full article content is not available on any tier. Production/commercial requires the Business plan at $449/month. This matches the historical restrictions you cited and fails three of your criteria.
- **Webz.io News API Lite still exists and is free:** "up to 30 days of historical data… up to 1,000 calls per month," issued via an emailed key, for non-commercial projects. The 1,000/**month** cap (~33/day) is below your daily polling need.
- **Guardian free developer key confirmed:** the Open Platform team cites **12 calls/sec and 5,000 calls/day**; the content/body field requires the key. (An older third-party figure of "500/day" appears outdated; the current documented figure is 5,000/day.)
- **GDELT DOC 2.0 confirmed:** no API key; enforces rate limiting with 429s and can block bursty IPs; now needs a `User-Agent` header.
- **GNews free tier confirmed:** per NewsData.io's tested 2026 roundup, "The free plan of the GNews API provides you with 100 requests per day, and you can make a maximum of one request per second. This Free News API can be used for development and testing only, as it does not support commercial projects."
- **NYT confirmed:** Article Search rate limits are "500 requests/day and 5 requests/minute" (NYT FAQ via the Georgetown library guide); the Archive API is "limited to 2,000 requests per day" (NYT public_api_specs on GitHub).

## Recommendations
**Stage 1 — Stand up the default now.** Implement **Currents API** as your primary keyword source: instant free key, 1,000/day, real-time, Boolean search across many outlets. Cache results and de-duplicate by URL/title. Add **The Guardian** as an immediate automatic secondary (it is the most reliable and has the most generous soft limits, and gives full body text for any story you want to read in depth).

**Stage 2 — Add depth and resilience.** Add **NewsData.io** as a tertiary aggregator (commercial-use-safe, 2,000 articles/day) — accept its 12-hour delay only for non-time-critical topics. Add **GDELT DOC 2.0** as a breadth/recency supplement, but *throttle it hard*: one request at a time, ≥1–2 seconds apart, a real `User-Agent` header, and exponential backoff on any 429.

**Stage 3 — Verticalize.** Route finance queries additionally to **Marketaux** (ticker + sentiment), tech queries to **Hacker News Algolia** (`search_by_date`), and space (if relevant) to **SNAPI**. Keep a small set of **direct RSS feeds** (Reuters/AP/BBC/Guardian) as a zero-dependency last-resort heartbeat.

**Fallback chain (degrade gracefully):**
`Currents → Guardian → NewsData.io → GDELT (throttled) → Marketaux/HN/SNAPI (vertical) → direct RSS`

On any `402/403/429` or 5xx from a source, fail over to the next and resume the primary on the next 15-minute cycle.

**Thresholds that would change these picks:**
- If you exceed ~1,000 req/day, drop Currents to secondary and promote Guardian (5,000/day) or move NewsData.io to a paid tier.
- If real-time is critical for finance markets, demote NewsData.io (12-h delay) and lean on Currents + Guardian + Marketaux.
- If the project becomes clearly commercial and redistributes article data, drop Currents and NYT (redistribution/non-commercial limits) and standardize on **NewsData.io** (commercial-friendly free) plus a paid Guardian or GNews plan.
- If GDELT starts returning frequent 429s at your volume, stop using it as anything but an occasional breadth query.

## Caveats
- **Skip for this use case:** **NewsAPI.org** (100/day + 24-h delay + dev-only), **GNews free** (100/day is below your 150–300/day floor, plus non-commercial), **mediastack free** (100/**month**, HTTP-only, 30-min delay, non-commercial), **Webz.io Lite** (1,000/**month** ≈ 33/day), **World News API** (50 points/day is very tight and needs a backlink on free), **Marketaux/NYT** as *general* sources (100–500/day and non-commercial/finance-only), **Brave Search API** (free tier removed February 2026, card now required), **Bing News Search** (retired), **Reddit** (pre-approval gate), and **Serper.dev** (2,500 credits are one-time, not a recurring free tier).
- **Numbers I could not fully verify from an official page (flagged):** TheNewsAPI's exact free-tier daily request and per-request article counts are not shown on its public pricing page and require signup to confirm. Currents' per-second/burst limit is not documented anywhere official — only the 1,000/day quota — so a hidden burst cap cannot be ruled out until you test post-signup. Google News RSS has no official API or published ToS for programmatic use; treat it as an unofficial, breakable endpoint.
- **Conflicting figures encountered:** Some third-party listings cite NewsData.io free as "500/day" and Guardian as "500/day"; the official sources are **200 credits/day** (NewsData.io) and **5,000 calls/day** (Guardian). NYT figures vary across community posts (4,000–10,000/day historically) but the current official guidance is **500/day, 5/min** for Article Search (2,000/day for the Archive API). Where conflicts existed I used the official/most-recent figure and noted it.
- **Security/operational gotchas:** Most of these APIs pass the key in the URL (`apiKey=`, `token=`, `api-key=`) — keep calls server-side and out of logs/version control. GDELT needs a `User-Agent` header or it returns nothing. Guardian's ToS limits caching its content to 24 hours. Currents requires attribution and restricts redistributing/storing its article data.
